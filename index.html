Treatment techniques

How treatment may be delivered

Treatment is selected after your assessment. The approaches below explain, in plain language, what may be used and why — your plan is always discussed with you first.

Manual Therapy

Hands-on techniques such as joint mobilisation and soft-tissue work, used to reduce stiffness and improve comfortable movement.

Therapeutic Exercises

Structured, progressive exercises chosen for your condition and ability, practised with correct technique and reviewed as you improve.

Electrotherapy

Modalities such as TENS or ultrasound may be used where clinically indicated, alongside exercise and manual therapy.

Confirm at consultation

Kinesiology Taping

Supportive taping that may help with comfort and movement during rehabilitation, applied where appropriate to your condition.

Confirm at consultation

Cupping Therapy

A traditional adjunct used by some practitioners to ease muscle tension. Suitability is discussed with you before it is used.

Confirm at consultation

Mobility and Flexibility Training

Guided work to restore comfortable range of motion in the joints and muscles that need it, progressed at your pace.

Strengthening Exercises

Progressive resistance work to build the strength that supports your joints, posture and daily activities.

Functional Rehabilitation

Practising the movements you need in real life — stairs, lifting, walking, getting up from the floor — with graded difficulty.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Answers to common questions about physiotherapy, your first visit and booking. If your question is not covered here, please call the clinic on +91 83101 94612.

Physiotherapy is a healthcare profession that helps people restore movement and function. A physiotherapist assesses how you move, identifies the tissues and structures involved, and uses a combination of hands-on techniques, exercise and advice to reduce discomfort and improve mobility. Care is always adapted to your condition, your goals and your day-to-day activities.

You may consider physiotherapy for persistent pain, stiffness, reduced movement, weakness, balance problems, sports injuries, or while recovering from surgery or a fracture. If you have sudden severe pain, chest pain, loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin area, or a recent significant injury, seek urgent medical care first rather than waiting for a physiotherapy appointment.

Physiotherapy is commonly used for back pain. An assessment helps identify contributing factors such as stiffness, weakness, posture or load, after which a plan of manual therapy, exercise and activity advice can be built. Results vary from person to person and depend on the cause of the pain, so no specific outcome is promised.

The number of sessions varies with the condition, how long it has been present, your general health and your goals. Your physiotherapist will discuss an expected plan with you after the initial assessment and review progress regularly, adjusting the plan as needed.

A prescription is not always required to begin physiotherapy, but some conditions do need medical clearance first. If you have had surgery, a fracture, or a significant medical history, please bring your doctor's advice and relevant reports so treatment can be planned safely.

Please bring previous prescriptions, X-ray, MRI or blood test reports if you have them, a list of your current medicines, and comfortable clothing that allows the area being treated to be seen and moved. If you use a walking aid, please bring it with you.

Sessions commonly last around 30 to 45 minutes, depending on the treatment and the exercises involved. Your first visit is usually longer because it includes a detailed assessment. The clinic will confirm timings when your appointment is booked.

Yes. Physiotherapy for older adults is adapted to individual strength, balance and medical conditions, and often focuses on walking, stairs, getting up from a chair and maintaining independence. Exercises are progressed gradually and safely.

Physiotherapy is often part of recovery after orthopaedic surgery, and programmes are usually based on the surgeon's protocol and stage of healing. Please confirm with your surgeon that physiotherapy may begin, and share the protocol with your physiotherapist.
